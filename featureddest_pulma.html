Why You Should Visit These Places

PALAWAN

Palawan is a top destination for tourists seeking dramatic limestone cliffs, turquoise lagoons, and white-sand beaches across over 1,700 islands. Visitors can kayak through El Nido's hidden coves, dive among World War II shipwrecks in Coron, or explore the UNESCO-listed Puerto Princesa Underground River. Beyond adventure, the province offers relaxed coastal towns like Port Barton and rich marine sanctuaries for unforgettable island hopping.

CEBU

a premier Philippine destination known as the "Queen City of the South," offering a vibrant blend of rich Spanish-colonial history, turquoise waters, and thrilling outdoor adventures. Tourists can swim alongside massive sardine runs in Moalboal, go canyoneering through the bright blue cascades of Kawasan Falls, or relax on the pristine white-sand beaches of Bantayan and Malapascua islands. In the bustling city center, visitors can also explore historic landmarks like Magellan's Cross and enjoy famous local dishes such as crispy Cebu lechon.

BOHOL

a captivating island destination famous for the iconic Chocolate Hills, a geological wonder of over 1,200 cone-shaped mounds that turn brown during the dry season. Tourists can spot the tiny, wide-eyed Philippine tarsier in protected sanctuaries, cruise along the lush Loboc River, and unwind on the white-sand beaches of Panglao Island. Beyond its scenic landscapes, Bohol also offers world-class diving at Balicasag Island and centuries-old coral stone churches rich in history.

BAGUIO

famously known as the "Summer Capital of the Philippines," offering tourists a refreshing escape with its cool mountain climate, towering pine trees, and mist-covered hills. Visitors can stroll and boat around Burnham Park, admire colorful hillside murals in the StoBoSa valley, and browse fresh produce and local delicacies at the bustling Baguio City Public Market. The city is also a vibrant hub for arts and culture, featuring attractions like the BenCab Museum, Mines View Park, and historic Camp John Hay.

Intramuros

brings Philippine history to life through its preserved 16th-century defensive walls, stone gates, and cobblestone streets that once served as the seat of Spanish colonial power. Landmarks like Fort Santiago trace the country's struggle for independence—most notably where national hero José Rizal was imprisoned before his execution—while museums like Casa Manila and Bahay Tsinoy showcase colonial-era lifestyles and Chinese-Filipino heritage. Through guided walking tours, interactive light shows, and surviving structures like the UNESCO-listed San Agustin Church, the district tells the story of colonization, World War II devastation, and Manila's enduring resilience.
